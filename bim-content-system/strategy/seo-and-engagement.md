## How search works on these platforms

Understanding this changes what you write.

| Platform | How content is found | What to optimise |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Feed algorithm first, internal search second, Google third. LinkedIn posts do get indexed by Google. | First 2–3 lines, dwell time, saves, meaningful comments. Keywords in the first 200 characters. |
| **Instagram** | Explore, hashtags, and increasingly caption keyword search | Keywords in the first line of the caption, alt text, hashtags |
| **TikTok** | Interest graph and on-screen text — TikTok reads text in video | On-screen text keywords, spoken keywords, caption |
| **Google** | Indexes LinkedIn posts, LinkedIn articles, and public profiles | Long-form articles, headline phrasing, profile copy |

`[Key point]` **Dwell time is the strongest LinkedIn signal you control.** A post that holds
someone for 40 seconds outperforms a post that gets more likes and is read in 5. This is why
posts in this system are 1–3 minutes long rather than short — and why the hook has to survive
the "see more" cut.

## Keyword clusters

Organised by search intent. Each cluster maps to weeks in the strategy.

### Cluster 1 — Definitional (highest volume, lowest competition from practitioners)

`what is BIM` · `BIM meaning` · `BIM definition` · `what does BIM stand for` ·
`building information modelling` · `BIM for beginners` · `BIM explained` · `is BIM software` ·
`BIM vs 3D model`

**Weeks:** 1, 2, 12 · **Format:** definitional posts and carousels
**Note:** these are dominated by software vendors and content farms. A practitioner answering
them properly, with sources, is genuinely differentiated.

### Cluster 2 — Comparative (high intent, high engagement)

`BIM vs CAD` · `Revit vs AutoCAD` · `difference between BIM and CAD` · `is AutoCAD dead` ·
`Revit or AutoCAD first` · `openBIM vs closed BIM` · `LOD vs level of information need` ·
`PIM vs AIM` · `link vs import Revit`

**Weeks:** 5, 45, 46 · **Format:** comparison tables, both-sides posts

### Cluster 3 — Standards (low volume, extremely high value audience)

`ISO 19650` · `ISO 19650 explained` · `EIR` · `exchange information requirements` ·
`BIM execution plan` · `what is a CDE` · `common data environment` · `OIR PIR AIR EIR` ·
`ISO 19650 vs BIM Level 2` · `IFC` · `COBie` · `EN 17412` · `level of information need`

**Weeks:** 27, 41–48 · **Format:** explainers and carousels
**Note:** low search volume, but the people searching are BIM managers, information managers,
and clients — exactly the audience that hires consultants.

### Cluster 4 — Contested (the differentiation cluster)

`6D BIM` · `7D BIM` · `what is 6D BIM` · `BIM dimensions explained` · `8D BIM` ·
`BIM levels` · `BIM Level 2` · `BIM maturity levels`

**Weeks:** 9, 10 · **Format:** carousels
**Note:** high volume, and the existing results are confidently contradictory. Being the account
that says "the industry does not agree, here are both positions, here are the sources" is the
single strongest positioning play available.

### Cluster 5 — Revit how-to (highest save rate)

`Revit schedules` · `Revit warnings` · `why is my Revit model slow` ·

**Weeks:** 13–26, 36–39 · **Format:** tutorials, tips, annotated screenshots

### Cluster 6 — Troubleshooting (highest urgency, highest save rate)

`Revit element not showing` · `Revit view range explained` · `Revit tag is blank` ·

**Weeks:** woven through Wednesday slots
**Note:** people search these while frustrated, at work, right now. They save the answer and

`BIM skills` · `BIM certification worth it` · `BIM portfolio` · `BIM freelance` ·

**Weeks:** Friday slots throughout · **Format:** career posts

## Posting times

**Base timezone: WAT (UTC+1).** Vollmann's audience spans West Africa, the UK, the EU, the Gulf,
and North America.

| Slot | WAT | Reaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary — Monday/Wednesday/Friday** | **07:30–08:30** | West Africa start of day · UK 06:30–07:30 (commute) · EU 08:30–09:30 (start of day) |
| **Secondary** | 12:30–13:30 | West Africa lunch · UK/EU lunch · Gulf afternoon |
| **Tertiary** | 16:00–17:00 | West Africa end of day · UK afternoon · US East Coast morning |

`[Practice]` **Best days on LinkedIn: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.** The Monday/Wednesday/Friday
rhythm in this system is chosen for **discipline over optimisation** — a fixed schedule you
actually keep beats an optimal schedule you abandon in week seven.

If you want to optimise later, shift to Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday once the habit is established
and measure the difference over a full quarter, not a fortnight.

**Instagram:** 12:00–13:00 and 19:00–21:00 WAT.
**TikTok:** 19:00–22:00 WAT.
**WhatsApp Status:** 07:00–09:00 and 20:00–22:00 WAT — start and end of the working day.

**The first 60 minutes matter most on LinkedIn.** Post when you can reply to comments for the
following hour. A post published and then ignored for a day performs materially worse than the
same post published when you are available.

## Engagement mechanics

### Questions that actually get answered

The difference between a question that generates comments and one that generates silence is
whether it is **specific, experiential, and low-risk to answer.**

| Works | Does not work |
|---|---|
| "What is the worst-structured model you have inherited?" | "Thoughts?" |
| "Have you seen 6D in a real tender — and did anyone define it?" | "Do you agree?" |
| "What are you still using CAD for?" | "What do you think about BIM?" |
| "Which of the nine steps does your organisation do worst?" | "Is BIM the future?" |
| "How did you answer when someone asked what BIM level you work at?" | "Let me know below!" |

**The pattern:** ask about **their experience**, not their opinion. Experience is easy to share
and impossible to be wrong about. Opinions invite silence because people fear being judged.

### Comment discipline

1. **Reply to every comment for the first 24 hours.** Non-negotiable while building.
2. **Reply with a sentence, not a word.** "Thanks" adds nothing to the thread.
3. **Ask a follow-up question** in your reply. This is the cheapest way to double a thread.
4. **When someone disagrees and is right, say so publicly.** It is the strongest credibility
   signal available and almost nobody does it.
5. **When someone disagrees and is wrong, cite the source calmly.** Never argue about tone.
6. **Never delete a critical comment** unless it is abusive. Deleting looks worse than being wrong.

### Conversion mechanics

The purpose is freelance, consulting, and training work. That requires making it possible to
know what you do and that you are available — without turning the feed into an advertisement.

| Mechanism | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **Profile headline states it plainly** | Permanent: "BIM Specialist · Autodesk Revit · Architectural Designer" |
| **Featured section** shows real work | Permanent, updated quarterly |
| **A Friday post that describes what you actually do** | Roughly monthly |
| **"My DMs are open" CTA** | Roughly monthly, never more |
| **An explicit availability post** | Once a quarter, maximum |
| **Lead magnet ("comment 'guide'")** | Once a quarter |

`[Judgement]` The ratio that works is roughly **11 posts that teach for every 1 that sells.**
Below that ratio the account stops being worth following. Above it, nobody knows you are
available and the whole exercise produces nothing.

## Measurement

Review quarterly, never weekly. Weekly numbers are noise.

| Tier | Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignore** | Impressions, likes, follower count alone | Vanity. Movable by luck. |
| **Watch** | Saves, shares, comments-per-post | The content is useful, not merely agreeable |
| **Watch** | Repeat commenters across weeks | You are building an audience, not catching a wave |
| **Act on** | Profile views in the 48 hours after posting | Pre-enquiry behaviour. The strongest leading indicator. |
| **Act on** | DMs about work | The actual goal |
| **Act on** | Inbound enquiries and their source | Which post or which platform is producing work |

### Quarterly diagnostic

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Good reach, no saves | Content is agreeable but not useful | More troubleshooting and tutorial content |
| Good saves, no profile views | Useful but impersonal | Strengthen the Friday professional slot |
| Profile views, no DMs | People do not know you are available | Add an availability post; check the headline and Featured section |
| Falling engagement across a quarter | Repetition, or the level has drifted too high | Return to Level 1 topics; check you are not repeating angles |
| Comments only from peers, never clients | Content is too technical for buyers | Add posts framed around cost, risk and programme, not software |

## Repurposing into longer assets

The 52-week library is designed to compound into products. After a full year you have the
material for:

| Asset | Built from |
|---|---|
| **Ebook: "BIM for Beginners"** | Q1 posts (weeks 1–12) plus the Level 1 knowledge base |
| **Ebook: "Revit from Zero"** | Q2 posts plus the Revit tutorial series |
| **Course: "BIM Coordination"** | Q3 posts plus Level 2 knowledge base |
| **Course: "ISO 19650 in Practice"** | Q4 posts plus Level 3 knowledge base |
| **Newsletter** | Weekly, assembled from the Monday post plus the Wednesday tip |
| **Workshop / CPD session** | Any single carousel, expanded with worked examples |
| **Blog / LinkedIn articles** | Monday posts expanded to 1,200–1,800 words with full citations |

`[Judgement]` Do not build any of these until the year is complete. Publishing the 52 weeks
first proves demand, produces the audience that will buy, and — most valuably — the comments
tell you exactly which sections need more depth.
