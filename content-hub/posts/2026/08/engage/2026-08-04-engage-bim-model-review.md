A model can look complete and still leave the site team answering avoidable questions.

When a Revit model moves toward issue, I review it as delivery information, not only as a three-dimensional object.

The useful questions are practical:

Is the information consistent across the model, views, schedules, and drawings?

Can the next person make a construction decision from what has been issued?

Which coordination issues are visible now, and which ones are likely to become site questions later?

Are the remaining actions clear enough for the project team to own and close?

The value of a BIM review is not adding complexity for its own sake. It is making the next decision visible before the information reaches the people who have to build from it.

This is the role of a focused BIM model and drawing review: a defined check of a live package, followed by a findings register, coordination priorities, and practical next steps.
