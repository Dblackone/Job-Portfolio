# Paid knowledge and recurring support offers

These are small, clearly bounded offers to test after the first marketplace or
referral conversation. They are not passive-income products and do not imply
that any offer has been published or sold.

## Offer 1: BIM package review session

### Best fit

A client or small team has a Revit model, drawing package, or coordination issue
and needs a focused review before issue, tender, or construction.

### Client receives

- A short review session based on the supplied package.
- A prioritized findings register.
- Clarifying questions and practical next steps.
- A written boundary statement identifying what was and was not reviewed.

### Client supplies

The relevant model or drawings, project stage, disciplines included, review
deadline, and the specific decision the review should support.

### Boundary

This is an independent documentation and coordination review. It is not design
approval, engineering certification, statutory sign-off, or a substitute for the
appointed architect or engineer.

### Qualification questions

1. What project stage is the package at?
2. Which disciplines and file formats are included?
3. What decision or issue must the review support?
4. What is the required review date?

## Offer 2: Construction reporting and procurement tracker setup

### Best fit

An active project has information spread across messages, spreadsheets, and
documents, and the team needs a usable reporting rhythm.

### Client receives

- A structured action and decision register.
- A progress-reporting template matched to the project stage.
- A procurement or package tracker based on supplied information.
- A short handover note explaining how to maintain the system.

### Client supplies

The current programme or progress information, procurement list, reporting
audience, existing templates, and the preferred spreadsheet or document format.

### Boundary

The tracker organizes supplied information; it does not independently verify
quantities, certify valuations, approve contracts, or replace professional cost,
commercial, or project-management authority.

### Qualification questions

1. Who will use the tracker and how often?
2. What information is currently missing or unreliable?
3. Which procurement packages or decisions are most urgent?
4. What reporting date or meeting must the first version support?

## Offer 3: Monthly BIM and delivery-information support

### Best fit

A practice, contractor, or project team needs a small recurring support block for
documentation review, issue tracking, reporting, or document control.

### Monthly scope examples

- Review an agreed number of drawing or model packages.
- Maintain a coordination issue register.
- Prepare a recurring progress or decision report.
- Maintain document-control and procurement records.
- Attend an agreed coordination or delivery meeting.

### Commercial structure

Start with a defined first month. State the included hours or outputs, response
times, meeting limit, revision allowance, client inputs, and exclusions. Review
the scope at month end before renewing.

### Boundary

Do not promise unlimited availability, professional sign-off, advanced Procore
implementation, licensed quantity surveying, or multidisciplinary design
responsibility.

### Qualification questions

1. What recurring problem is consuming the team's time?
2. What outputs must exist at the end of each month?
3. Who supplies and approves the source information?
4. What response time and meeting rhythm are actually required?

## Short invitation copy

I offer focused BIM review, construction-reporting setup, and monthly delivery-
information support. Each engagement starts with the project stage, available
files, required outputs, deadline, assumptions, and review boundaries. Share the
current information problem and the next decision it must support; I will confirm
whether a defined review, setup, or recurring scope is appropriate.

## Conversion rule

Offer the smallest paid scope that resolves a real, clearly described problem.
Only propose the recurring option after the first assignment reveals a repeatable
need and the client accepts the initial deliverable.
